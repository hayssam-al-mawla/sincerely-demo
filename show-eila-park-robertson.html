The Sincerely Show, with Eila Park Robertson

The Price of Peace Is Pain. There Is No Other Way Through.

She stood up from the floor in Vancouver, took out her phone, pressed record, and finally said everything she had never been allowed to say.

July 3, 2026, 1 hr 16 min

  • Climate
  • Racial justice
  • Storytelling
  • Voice
Listen on Spotify Apple Podcasts Watch on YouTube

Katherine Dudtschak sits down with Eila Park Robertson, former ABC News journalist, award-winning filmmaker, crisis communications strategist, and founder of Anecdotia, a production agency focused on racial, social, and climate justice storytelling. Eila led communications for the City of Portland during over 100 consecutive nights of civil unrest, navigating daily racial harassment, hundreds of death threats, and a period of personal crisis she speaks about with complete honesty.

What she built from that experience is a mission: to help progressive governments, advocates, and organisations communicate in ways that actually reach the people they most need to reach. Her central argument is simple and urgent. Calling people out has failed. Calling people in is the only thing that works.

This conversation covers her Korean immigrant parents, her violent father, the seven-minute video she posted after the Atlanta spa shootings that changed everything, the husband who fell in love with her when she could see nothing worth loving in herself, and what she now knows with certainty: the price of peace is pain, and there is no shortcut through it.

“The price of peace is pain. There is no shortcut. There is no other way.”Eila Park Robertson
