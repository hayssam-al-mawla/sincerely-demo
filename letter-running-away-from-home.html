Running away from home…

November 3, 2026

Remember wanting to run away from home? To escape something?

There is a pattern I’ve come to recognise in the human journey. For many of us, it seems we spend the first half of our lives running away from home, from the child we once were, and the second half running home.

I’ve been curious about the human condition for as long as I can remember. About who we are, the lives we inherit, the lives we create, and how we find our place in this world. Over the past decade, I’ve been on what feels like a journey of transformation.

Yet I wonder. Has it really been a journey of transformation, or have I been finding my way home?

Some of my earliest and happiest memories are of being a deeply curious, creative, sensitive and playful child. And a little mischievous, too!

My mother called me Snoopy because I was into absolutely everything. When I was three, I took apart our radio, looking for the little man whose voice was coming out of it. I was terribly disappointed when I couldn’t find him!

I loved anything mechanical. Tractors, cars, trucks, motorcycles. I loved driving the tractor and cutting the grass on our farm, enjoying the solitude and making something look beautiful. I built forts and clubhouses with my sister, explored the little forest near our home, and created imaginary worlds of my own.

One of those worlds was in an old root cellar beside the barn. Part of the wall had fallen in, leaving sand that became the landscape for my very own farm. I also built farms in the upper level of the barn. I made roads and long fence lines with corn cobs that the rats had stripped of their kernels, and drove my toy tractor around the little farms I had created.

Oh, how I loved imagining and creating things!

I was also drawn to beautiful things. I loved exploring my mother’s jewellery, trying on her earrings and clothes, and making little outfits for my sister’s Barbie doll. I loved singing, drawing, painting and pottery. I loved skipping with the girls at school. There were feminine and masculine qualities within me that I didn’t yet have the language to understand. They were all simply part of who I was.

Of course, childhood wasn’t all adventure and play.

My parents carried the experiences of war, hardship and survival into our family. They worked incredibly hard to build a life in Canada, and our humble mink farm brought its own financial pressures and challenges. There was love, but there was also anxiety, fear and the emotional weight of experiences I wouldn’t begin to understand until much later in life.

School was difficult for me, too. I learned differently, and I struggled to understand why the very things that came naturally to me weren’t always valued in the world around me.

Somewhere along the way, I began to learn what was acceptable. I remember thinking, “Adults don’t do that. Good people don’t do that.” Little by little, parts of that curious, expressive, feminine, playful child began to disappear from view.

I became determined. Determined to prove that I was smart enough, capable enough, good enough. I wanted to build a better life than my parents had known and create opportunity and security for myself and the people I loved.

And in so many ways, I did.

I’m deeply grateful for my life. Four extraordinary children, dear friendships and a career that took me to places I could never have imagined as a child on that farm. So much love, joy, learning and meaningful contribution. I wouldn’t trade those experiences or the people who shared them with me.

Yet I can also see how good I became at adapting, and how much of myself I learned to hide or dismiss. Some of those hidden parts carried guilt, shame and fear.

Over the past decade, so much of my journey has been about letting go of that guilt and shame and pressure to perform, and learning to love the qualities of the child I once was. The child inside of me.

Affirming my gender was a profound part of that journey, but it has been about so much more. My sensitivity, my curiosity, my sense of adventure, my femininity, my appreciation of beauty, my creativity, my playfulness. Even my mischievousness!

I’ve come to appreciate that the child I once was didn’t need to become someone better. She needed to be understood, welcomed and loved. And I needed to learn to love her myself.

I’ve long believed that we arrive in this world perfect, whole and complete, with an essence that is already beautiful and full of love. It comes from somewhere deep within us, and perhaps from somewhere beyond us. We have lots to learn and experience in this lifetime, but perhaps we don’t need to become someone else to be worthy of this life we’ve been given.

As I listen to the stories of others, particularly through The Sincerely Show, I recognise how many people have experienced their own version of this journey. Some have travelled a long way from the child they once were, while others seem to have remained much closer to the things they loved and the ways they naturally expressed themselves.

It makes me wonder what kind of world we might create if more of us felt safe and loved enough to remain close to those beautiful parts of ourselves throughout our lives. Perhaps we wouldn’t have to travel so far, or experience such painful crashes and crumbling, to find our way home.

I smile when I think about where my own journey has brought me.

Here I am in midlife, living on a small farm again. I ride my tractor, walk the fields and tend the gardens, surrounded by nature and beauty. I’m creating things, exploring, learning, playing and enjoying the freedom to express all of who I am.

The farm I’m living on today is so close to the very farm I imagined and built as a little child, with sand, corn cobs and my toy tractor.

Only now, it’s real.

I carry decades of living, loving, learning, accomplishments and heartbreak. I wouldn’t wish those years away. I cherish the life I’ve lived and the people who have shared it with me.

But how beautiful to be doing so many of the things I loved as a child again, with the freedom to be me. All of me.

I think about that little child walking through the fields, full of questions and wonder, with no idea of the life that lay ahead. And here I am, all these years later, walking the fields again. Still curious, still sensitive, still playful, still learning.

Only now, home. Home in myself.

It feels like we spend the first half of our lives running away from home, and the second half running home.

How beautiful is that?

Sincerely, Katherine.
