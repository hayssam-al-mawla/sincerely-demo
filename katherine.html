Katherine Dudtschak

Founder and CEO of Sincerely. Author of Sincerely, Katherine. Host of The Sincerely Show. A former bank CEO whose own transformation became the seed of an institute.

Invite Katherine to speak Read the memoir

Before you hear my story, let me tell you who I am underneath it.

I am curious. Endlessly curious. As a child, I took apart our radio looking for the man inside it.

I am playful. I believe joy is not a reward for work well done but the very ground we’re meant to walk on.

I am someone who sees patterns. My mind moves differently. It took me years to understand this as a gift rather than a flaw.

I am soft now, in ways I couldn’t be before. And I am still learning. Still growing. Still becoming.

From a farm in rural Canada to a farm of her own

One profound personal transformation and three major leadership transformations. Distinct experiences, deeply connected.

  1. The farmChildhood, rural Ontario

    Eight acres and a secret

    Katherine grew up on a humble farm with a red-brick farmhouse and a red hip-roof barn, the daughter of German immigrants whose lives had been shaped by war and displacement. From as far back as she can remember she knew who she was on the inside, and she kept that secret for nearly fifty years.

  2. 13Her first job

    A mind that works in patterns

    Her report cards said she should focus more. Nobody knew she was dyslexic. She started working at thirteen on a neighbour’s farm, put herself through school, married, had four children in four years, and built a banking career that would span nearly four decades.

  3. 2013CEO, RBC Caribbean

    Saving a bank and its livelihoods

    She led operations across 19 countries through a consequential turnaround. Candour, compassion and trust anchored the renewal, and sustainable profitability returned in about 24 months.

  4. 2015Executive Vice President, Regional Banking, RBC

    Twenty-five thousand colleagues

    Responsible for roughly a third of RBC’s workforce and more than 15 million clients, she transformed the nature of the work with deep attention to inclusion, care and advisor capability.

  5. 2019Coming home to herself

    She affirmed her gender to 80,000 colleagues

    She recorded a video for the whole bank and expected rejection. What began in fear unfolded into love, acceptance and standing ovations. Colleagues began entrusting her with their own stories, which deepened her understanding of the burdens people carry and the potential they hold.

  6. 2022Catalyst Honours

    Business Leader Champion

    Recognized for inclusive leadership that enabled exceptional, sustained business performance. She went on to lead HomeEquity Bank as CEO, where the asset base grew past C$10 billion.

  7. TodayMaison Millefleurs, Prince Edward County

    Sincerely

    She left banking, found a farm, planted lavender and welcomed bees. For the first time in her life she wasn’t performing. From there she founded Sincerely, hosts The Sincerely Show, writes Sincerely Letters, and works with a small number of leaders and institutions.

What the journey taught me

The strongest leaders I knew were the ones who had integrated both. Disciplined and present. Strategic and intuitive. Courageous and kind.

Sacred Structure

Discipline. Achievement. Strategy. Command. The ability to see systems and move through them with precision. These are gifts. I honour them. They are my foundation.

Sacred Flow

Softness. Slowness. Feeling. The part that wanted to grieve and love and create and connect. The part that knew truth lives in the body, not just the mind.

When I finally embraced both, the two parts of me stopped fighting. They began to dance. I call this integration the Athena way, after the goddess who held both warrior and wisdom.

What I know

This knowing is not ego. It comes from experience, from seeing, from a lifetime of living across worlds.

  • I believe human beings are whole. Not broken. Not problems to be solved.
  • I know most of us have simply forgotten.
  • I know the power of stories. When we hear someone else’s honest story, we see ourselves more clearly.
  • I know the world’s outer crises are mirrors of our inner imbalance.
  • I know sincerity is not softness. It is the most courageous thing left to us.
  • And I know there is room for everyone at this table.

The work before

Her life and leadership are not separate sources of credibility. Together they form the foundation of Sincerely.

Katherine serves on the board of the Canadian Museum for Human Rights. She is a citizen of Canada and the European Union, and a mother of four.

The recognition she values most cannot be measured: the colleagues who told her that her vulnerability gave them permission to be real.
