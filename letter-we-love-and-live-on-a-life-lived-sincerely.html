A letter from Katherine, September 29, 2026.

We love and live on: a life lived sincerely.

September 29, 2026

A tribute to Cindy Clark of team Sincerely and her son, the late Corey Clark, September 26th, 1992 – December 29th, 2007.

Earlier this spring, Cindy Clark reached out to me. She had heard about my book and Sincerely and wondered if there was a way she could help. She was drawn to the mission of Sincerely.

Cindy and I had worked for RBC, Canada’s largest bank, for several decades, our careers crossing paths many times. Whenever I held a round table or a town hall and Cindy was there, I felt her warmth. There was a kindness in her presence, an optimism and curiosity that she brought into the room. I always felt her desire to bring something good to the lives of others. To help unlock the gifts, the talents in others.

Like me, Cindy had left RBC to create another chapter in her life. We had both lived and worked in different places across this part of Canada, and here we were, our paths crossing again in Prince Edward County.

When we reconnected over coffee, I felt that same loving, passionate energy. A call to serve, a desire to care and to give. As we talked, Cindy shared with me the story of her son, Corey.

September 26th marks the day Corey would have turned 36 years old. Unfortunately he passed away at the age of 17. He had lived with osteosarcoma, an aggressive form of bone cancer, for four years. It was the same incredibly painful cancer that Terry Fox battled.

Cindy told me about the work she and Corey had done with healers to help him endure his pain and rise above it, quite literally. She also told me that, while still a teenager, he had written his own eulogy. So young, and so thoughtful, thinking so deeply about life and what he wanted to leave with and for others.

In a piece she wrote about Corey, Cindy describes his wish to inspire or help at least one person in his lifetime. At seventeen, he had begun journalling about what he was learning, hoping others might learn from it, too. She shares his own words about those four years:

“There’s a whole bunch of details I could get in to but I don’t feel like writing it. Just to give you an idea about what I’ve been through, I have a lot of metal in me, I have six really long scars, and I know for sure I hate hospital food. Over the past 4 years I’ve had some of the worst days of my life and I’ve had some of the best days in my life. I’ve also learned a lot about life!”

It’s special and inspiring to be able to hear his humour in those words. And his honesty about the life he was living, with its worst days and its best days. Cindy speaks of his belief in a positive attitude, in treating people with kindness and respect, and in learning someone’s story before judging them. Her memories let us see how he lived those beliefs.

She remembers a woman who approached the family at a fundraiser and told them that Corey used to buy her pizza when she was hungry. An intern at the hospital shared how they had learned to cast because Corey let them put casts on all his fingers as he lay in bed after surgery.

Cindy also shares the story of the boat he chose as his Children’s Wish Foundation gift, so that he could share good times with his friends. A bridge he used to take them to now honours his memory. There was such generosity in the way he wanted others to experience life with him.

And then there is this memory from his best friend’s wedding, on August 22nd, 2025, eighteen years after Corey had made his request. In Cindy’s own words:

“I was honoured to dance with his best friend at his wedding, a request Corey had made years earlier because he wanted me to have that experience as the mother of the groom.”

I find myself sitting in tears with this dance. Corey had imagined something for his mother, and years later she stepped into it. His best friend had held that wish and made it part of his own wedding. There is such tenderness in the way love can reach forward into a life, and be there to meet us.

Cindy shares that he “wanted to ensure that his legacy was not one of sickness but one of his amazing life.” Through these stories, I am getting to know something of that life, and of the extraordinary mother and family who love him.

When we met for coffee, Cindy also told me about an encounter she had after Corey’s passing. She was driving north of home when an eagle or a hawk came alongside her car, flying at window height. It stayed beside her for what felt like an eternity. In the depth of her being, Cindy knew this was Corey coming to see her, letting her know he would always be with her.

She has experienced many other remarkable moments like this, each reminding her of Corey’s spirit and his presence in her life. As she shares:

“His spirit is all around of us all of the time and we are so truly blessed to have him in our lives and incredibly proud.”

I find myself sitting with what it means to remain with one another. Cindy and Corey’s relationship continues to unfold. And now, through Cindy, I am coming to know him, too. His words are finding me. His wish to help someone is finding another expression through this letter.

Perhaps this is what I am beginning to feel in the words we love and live on. Through the love Cindy shares, Corey’s life becomes part of another life. There is still something being given and received.

It is an honour to have Cindy as part of team Sincerely, bringing the warmth and care I have felt in her presence for so many years. I welcome Corey’s spirit and presence as part of our team and mission, too, with the lessons and energy he shares directly and through his mother.

Cindy, thank you for sharing your beautiful son with us, and for all that you bring of yourself.

We love and live on.

Corey, a life lived sincerely.

Sincerely, Katherine.
