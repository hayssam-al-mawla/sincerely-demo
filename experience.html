The Sincerely Show

Every week Katherine sits with someone who has a story to tell. The conversations are long, unscripted and true.

Some guests transformed organizations. Some walked through loss and emerged softer. Some carried secrets for decades and finally told the truth. All of them found their way home to themselves, and are willing to share how.

Latest conversation, with Leah Thomas

How we treat the Earth mirrors how we treat each other

Leah is an environmental author and advocate working where climate meets culture. She and Katherine talk about the hidden story of Nicodemus, Kansas, about Earth Sessions and nature as her church, and about how radical imagination changed the question she asks of a broken system.

For anyone worn down by the weight of fixing the world.

Sincerely Letters

Once a week, from Katherine's desk to your inbox.

Sometimes it is a reflection from the week just lived. Sometimes it is something she heard, or noticed, or could not put down. Sometimes it is simply what she saw from the window, out over the lavender.

The black sheep.

This week's letter, November 10, 2026

A few days ago, I was talking with a friend, someone I care about and deeply admire. We were talking about family, about the things we carry through our lives, and about her journey of breaking a cycle of intergenerational trauma.

At one point, she referred to herself as the black sheep of the family.

Those words stopped me. I have never seen her as a black sheep.

Sincerely, Katherine.

The memoir

Sincerely, Katherine.

Life, gender, inclusivity and leadership for the future. The book that began it all.

It is a story of a life built on achievement and nearly lost to silence, of transformation that began with terror and ended with standing ovations. It is Katherine's story, and perhaps a mirror for your own.

A story in three parts

  1. Part one

    The journey

    A child on a farm, carrying a secret she couldn't name. A determined climber, building a life that looked right but felt wrong. A woman who, after fifty years, chose truth over safety.

  2. Part two

    The perspective

    What decades in leadership taught her. How organizations can become places of belonging, and what it means to lead from wholeness instead of fear.

  3. Part three

    The beginning

    Not an ending but an opening. The life that becomes possible when we stop performing and start being, and an invitation to your own becoming.
